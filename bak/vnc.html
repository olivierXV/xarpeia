VBN News Channel · Xareionople

The news, held in trust.

VNC is the Federation’s public news service: television on channel 2 and, with VRS, the VBN Radio Service, the radio beside it. It was founded in 164 AAX from the old newsroom of VBN Pontes and is run by a trust that no minister, owner or advertiser appoints.

    Parliament

    Both chambers, in plain terms.

    The rule books of the Federal Assembly allow the public broadcaster to film its sittings, and VNC does. Parliament Tonight, at 21:00, turns the day into thirty minutes anyone can follow.

    Who films what

    Sittings of the Federal Assembly, the Senate and the House of Representatives are filmed by VNC.

    Sittings of the Commonwealth Congress are filmed by the Commonwealth Broadcasting Service, the joint public broadcaster created by the Charter. It is a separate service with its own board, seated in Latreionople and Solaria. Parliament Tonight carries its pictures when the Congress sits.

    Radio

    The VBN Radio Service.

    VRS is VNC’s radio sister: news on the hour, long-form talk, music of record and programmes in the regional languages. It grew from the radio side of VBN’s old state-chartered newsroom and sits in the VNC Trust beside the channel.

    Before 141 AAX the same service name, VRS Triumterra, carried VBN’s radio on Regnum. Triumterra seized it in the revolt and it no longer broadcasts under VBN. VRS in the Federation is unrelated to what Triumterra now airs.

    Independence

    Three promises in the statute.

    VNC exists because the Third Republic decided never to repeat the years when a government could lean on VBN’s newsroom. Article 43 of the Constitution requires a public service funded by the federal budget, governed by an independent board appointed by the Civil Service Commission, with its editorial independence protected.

    No appointments

    VBN Corporation names no trustee, editor or director of VNC or VRS and has no vote on who does. Nor does any minister: the board is appointed by the Civil Service Commission.

    Guaranteed carriage

    VNC and VRS are carried on VBN transmitters at VBN’s cost, in full and at their chosen times, as long as VBN holds a licence.

    Open record

    The Trust publishes an annual report on editorial independence. VBN Corporation prints it unedited, with the reply of anyone it names.

    Like every broadcaster, VNC answers to the Federal Media Authority, the independent regulator appointed by the Civil Service Commission under Article 42.

    Our record

    From VBN Pontes to VNC.

    The service is new. The idea is old, and it has failed and been abused before it worked.

    1. The InterregnumVBN Pontes

      A first attempt at shared news

      An early radio service to bind the states together through trusted news collapses for lack of funding and a public that did not yet care.

    2. c. 58–75 AAXRevival

      The bridge is built

      VBN Pontes is revived as an institutional service for Filipinic Baybay, Leyan and Prado, the Germanic east and the Slavic borders. It succeeds once television spreads.

    3. 58 AAXArticle 27

      A charter, and no firewall

      The Second Republic calls VBN its principal broadcast institution and carrier of VBN Pontes, and protects its newsroom from nothing.

    4. 133–136 AAXPressure

      The newsroom leaned on

      Emergency powers pressure VBN’s editorial independence. The Oversight Committee is purged, then suspended.

    5. 141–155 AAXImperial Republic

      Compelled acclamation

      The newsroom airs what it is told. It remembers, and the Third Republic writes Article 43 so that it cannot happen again.

    6. 15 Dodeka, 155 AAXThe Swan Signal

      A ballet that would not end

      When the Emperor dies, the stations loop a ballet for days. The newsroom was among them.

    7. 164 AAXFounded

      VNC and the VNC Trust

      The old state-chartered newsroom becomes VNC, with VRS, in a statutory trust funded from the federal budget and governed by a board the Civil Service Commission appoints.
