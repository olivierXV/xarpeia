The Federal Assembly

The Assembly is the Federation's legislature. It has two chambers of equal constitutional standing: the House of Representatives, elected by proportional representation, and the Senate, which speaks for the States. No bill becomes law without both.

Articles 14–15

How a bill becomes law

Any member of either chamber, or the Cabinet, may introduce a bill. It must then pass both chambers by a simple majority and survive the President’s fifteen days.

  1. Introduced

    By any representative, any senator or the Cabinet. Budget bills must start in the House.

  2. First chamber

    A simple majority passes the bill and sends it across.

  3. Second chamber

    A simple majority again. The Senate may amend a budget bill but may not start one.

  4. The President

    Fifteen days to sign, or to return it with objections. Silence while the Assembly sits makes it law.

  5. Override

    A veto falls if two thirds of both chambers vote to override it.

  6. Review

    The Constitutional Court can void a law on a petition from one third of either chamber.

A bill whose veto has been sustained may not return in identical form in the same parliamentary term (Art. 15).

House of Representatives · Art. 13, 17

The House

Voters choose parties, and seats follow each party’s share of the vote within each State. Twenty percent of the seats are set aside for sectoral representatives elected by party list. They come from labour, peasants, the urban poor, indigenous cultural communities, women, youth, Eulons and omegas. The Federal Electoral Commission runs those elections.

  • Receives the draft budget 90 days before the fiscal year and passes it at least 30 days beforeArt. 16
  • Needs two thirds to pass a budget with a deficit over 3% of GDPArt. 16

  • Removes a Control Commissioner by two thirds, after a Supreme Court finding of misconductArt. 25
  • Impeaches a federal official by two thirdsArt. 26

Senate · Art. 13

The Senate

Every State sends two senators, whatever its size. Terms last six years and one third of the chamber is renewed every two years, so the Senate never faces a general election and cannot be dissolved. The ten eastern States together hold twenty seats.

Senators sit by State delegation, so a State’s two senators sit together even when they belong to different parties. The party makeup follows the 164 AAX election.

Articles 14, 36, 53

What the two chambers do together

These powers belong to the Assembly as a whole. Most need a majority in each chamber.

  • Federal taxation, budget, public debt and appropriationsArt. 14
  • Declaration of war and ratification of every treatyArt. 14
  • Citizenship, immigration and federal criminal lawArt. 14
  • Admission of new States and changes to State boundaries, by two thirdsArt. 2, 14
  • Federal election law, including campaign financeArt. 14, 17
  • Amends the Constitution by two thirds of both chambers, then a national referendumArt. 53
  • Ratifies the Commonwealth Charter by two thirdsArt. 49
  • Renews a national emergency in 30-day steps, by simple majorityArt. 31
  • Oversees the intelligence services through a Standing Committee from both chambers and all major partiesArt. 36
  • One third of either chamber may ask the Constitutional Court to review a lawArt. 20
  • Must be notified within seven days of any Commonwealth ruling that affects citizens’ rightsArt. 51
Presiding officers

Who runs each chamber

House

Speaker of the House

Lorraine Dominguez

Elected by the House and seated above party, she presides over debate and speaks for the chamber.

A former Social Progressive Party member elected from Baybay’s list, she left the party on taking the chair. She spent two decades in the Baybay legislature, where she was known for drafting the State’s first public-records law, and she sat on the Interim Council’s committee on legislative rules in 162 AAX.

House · United Conservatives

Leader of the Opposition

Ruy Duca

Leads the United Conservatives, the largest party outside the governing coalition.

Elected from Heliosberg’s list, he left public life under the Imperial Republic rather than serve it, which spared him the stain that clings to many conservatives. He rebuilt the party after 160 AAX around fiscal discipline and the States’ reserved powers, and he led the Heliosberg delegation in opposing the confirmation of Mateo von Matthews.

Senate · Centre Democratic Party

President of the Senate

Vincenzo Dmitrov

Elected by the senators. The Chief Justice, not the President of the Senate, presides at an impeachment trial.

A senator for Alexagrad, he served as a State official on the Nicolatian frontier before the Civil War and is known for patient work on judicial and commission confirmations. He keeps the Senate’s calendar and chairs its negotiations with the House over amended bills.
