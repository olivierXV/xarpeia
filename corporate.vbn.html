VBN Corporation · Xareionople

The signal that binds the states.

VBN is Latrevia's oldest private broadcaster: three television networks, a family of radio stations, and the studio that makes the programmes they carry. We began in the age of radio and we have stayed on the air through exile, empire and war.

Where it all began.

VBN was a radio company before it was anything else. Today three services carry that line: two of ours, and one that belongs to the Trust.

Radio

Radio 1

New for 166 AAX: the Corporation's own radio service. Talk, music, sport and the big evenings of VBN 1, simulcast and extended. It shares programming with Kiss FM at weekends.

Kiss FM

Youth music and chat, and the radio sister of V3. On the air since before 131 AAX, when a teenage DJ calling himself VX was one of its voices. Kiss FM keeps its own loud, bright identity and signs off every break as a VBN station.

The VBN Radio Service, the radio side of our old state-chartered newsroom. The serious service: news, long-form talk, music of record and programmes in the regional languages. VRS sits in the VNC Trust beside VNC and is as independent of this Corporation as its sister channel.

The island's station. We first reached Paradeisos when it was still called the Colony, and we have kept the old name in our archive.

Private subsidiary

LPN — Luisara People's Network

A VBN subsidiary inside Luisara, owned by the Corporation, operating under Luisaran law and out of reach of Commonwealth rules. It is a private network and not a public broadcaster. Its colours are those of the jungle sunrise. Spanish-language, with regional programming for each of the Republic's regions.

Partner broadcaster

NPBC — Nicolates Public Broadcasting Channel

NPBC carries selected VBN programmes under a standing carriage agreement. Titles are chosen by NPBC and subject to Nicolatian broadcasting law.

Station & transmitter

VBN Nikolatsk

Our bureau and transmitter in the Nicolatian capital region, in the south-west of the country. It works under licence and carries entertainment and a Latrevian-language service. There is no Nicolatian newsroom, and the Committee for Tsarist Security takes its usual interest.

Formerly on air

VBN Colline and VRS Triumterra

Before 141 AAX we ran VBN Colline (television) and VRS Triumterra (the VBN Radio Service) on Regnum. Triumterra seized both in the revolt of 138–141 AAX, and nothing airs there under our name today. Regnum is a separate planet: radio cannot cross the sun, so everything travels by portal, and end-ships can sail only at the two windows a year. We do not operate a station there.

Our record

What we broadcast, and when.

A broadcaster that has been on the air for more than two and a half centuries has been on the air on the worst days as well. This is our own account, including the years we would rather not have lived.

  1. c. 105 PAXRadio age

    The first transmitters

    Radio matures; the House of Vernalius puts up a transmitter and a company around it. VBN is a family business from its first broadcast, and remains one.

  2. The InterregnumBefore television

    A first attempt at shared news

    An early plan to bind the states together through one trusted radio news service collapses for want of money and an audience that did not yet care. The idea outlives the attempt.

  3. 13 PAXSuccession

    Adrastus V dies

    The Corporation has been run for years by Adrastus V as a private citizen. His son Xarpaii, newly Lord Mayor of Jumongrad, builds his influence in part through our airtime.

  4. 3 PAXOwnership

    The family steps back

    Xarpaii is elected President, and continued family ownership of a broadcaster becomes untenable. The Corporation passes to the branch of Olifhar Vernalius, where it remains.

  5. 31 Dodeka, 1 PAXVOS Day

    The transmitter taken

    Within three hours of the shots on the balcony, our transmitter is broadcasting something other than news. Television, invented in secret around this day, may have first carried the parade itself. No recording survives.

  6. 1–55 AAXThe exile

    Radio on the Gulf of Lancia

    Television is shelved by the war economy. Radio is cheap and carries across water; from the hill above Lancia the Corporation keeps a scattered, displaced people supplied with a signal.

  7. c. 52–58 AAXTelevision

    The picture returns

    Television is rebuilt as a mass medium, and VBN is one of its first regular broadcasters. Colour follows within a generation.

  8. c. 58–75 AAXVBN Pontes

    The bridge is built

    VBN Pontes is revived as an institutional service, not a personal project, to carry shared and trusted news to the Filipinic states, the Germanic east and the Slavic borderlands. A population that has lived through total war together is finally ready for it.

  9. 58 AAXSecond Republic

    A charter, and no firewall

    The Second Republic names VBN its principal broadcast institution under Article 27, with a state charter and private ownership at once, and nothing to protect the newsroom from the Cabinet. That gap is remembered.

  10. By 131 AAXKiss FM

    A youth station on the dial

    Kiss FM is on the air, with a teenage DJ called VX among its voices, working our tape library and schedule desk every night.

  11. 133–136 AAXPressure

    Independence squeezed

    Under emergency powers the Corporation's editorial independence is pressured. The Civil Service Oversight Committee is purged, then suspended. VBN's news is the next thing in the way.

  12. c. 136 AAXA second name

    Lumen Television

    To keep programmes on the air that the authorities would otherwise shut, VBN supplies Lumen Television, LTV, a second licence under another name. It is a thin disguise and everybody knows it, but it keeps a signal alive. LTV went off the air at the end of the civil war and returned on 21 Ennia 166 AAX, under Meridian, as a competitor.

  13. 138–141 AAXRegnum

    Colline and Triumterra lost

    Our television and radio stations on Regnum, VBN Colline and VRS Triumterra, are seized in the revolt. The portals are sealed in 141 and nothing airs there under our name again.

  14. 141–155 AAXThe Imperial Republic

    "Vivat Imperator"

    Under the Chancellor's control, the Corporation airs the hollow, compelled acclamation: eternal where the people's own cry had been return and memory. We broadcast it. We say so here.

  15. 15 Dodeka, 155 AAXThe Swan Signal

    A ballet that would not end

    When the Emperor dies, television stations loop a ballet for days, a silent admission that control has been lost. Ours was among them.

  16. 164 AAXThe Third Republic

    The newsroom goes into trust

    Under Article 43, our old state-chartered newsroom becomes VNC, and VNC, with VRS, is placed in the statutory VNC Trust: funded by the federal budget, governed by a board the Civil Service Commission appoints, carried by the Corporation and owned by no one in it. The other arrangement had been tried.

  17. 166 AAXToday

    Three networks, three radio voices, one signal

    VBN 1, V3, Radio 1 and Kiss FM for the Corporation; VNC and VRS for the Trust; LPN, our private subsidiary in Luisara, and our partners in Nicolates. The wordmark is unchanged, and the pixel star is back.

Corporate

Who owns what we say.

VBN Corporation is privately held by the Vernalius family, in the line of Olifhar Vernalius. It is a commercial company, funded by advertising and by licensing the work of VBN Productions, and it answers to its board, its shareholders and its licences.

It is regulated like any other broadcaster: no corporation may control more than a set share of the national audience in any one medium (Article 42), and the Federal Media Authority, whose members the Civil Service Commission appoints, is our regulator. VBN is also barred from owning, or being owned by, a political party, which matters in a family with our history.

It is not the state's broadcaster. The Corporation takes no position in the affairs of any royal house, ministry or party, and it does not hold the newsroom that tells you about them.

The VNC Trust

Three promises we cannot unmake.

These are written into the statute that founded the Trust, not into this website.

No appointments

The Corporation names no trustee, editor or director of VNC or VRS, and has no vote on who does.

Guaranteed carriage

VNC and VRS are carried on our transmitters at our cost, in full and at their chosen times, for as long as we hold a licence.

Open record

The Trust publishes its own annual report on editorial independence. We publish it here, unedited, and carry the response of anyone it names.

Our marks

Four marks, one house.

The wordmark

Our name and our principal mark, unchanged for generations.

The signal

A diamond and three pairs of chevrons, a signal travelling outward. As old as the wordmark itself; it closes our broadcasts and opens this page.

The check

The old V, a check mark with a long reach. Retired as our main logo, it survives inside the VBN Productions mark.

The pixel star

The Corporation's oldest device, a star and a trail of light. It was retired long ago and is brought back, redrawn in pixels, as the Corporation's signature for the new era.
